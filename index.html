Welcome

“Physics has never existed, and will never exist.” — Yang Dong, The Three-Body Problem

In Liu Cixin’s novel, the laws of physics stop working. This book asks how far that is from our own world, and how to live with that.

Science is not as absolute as one might think, even though it is taught as absolute facts in the curriculum. Behind the novel’s alien invasion, we follow real scientists through history as they keep hitting walls: broken symmetries, the problem of induction, chaos, the untamable weather. Reaching a wall is real progress but also a lesson in humility: our natural laws, it turns out, are not as lawful as we thought. The book ends with suggestions to live with the uncertainty that remains: “cautious confidence.”

Inside

  • Part I, When Laws Fail: what laws rest on, and where they give way.
  • Part II, Modeling the Unlawful: from ancient sky charts to climate models, predicting a world that won’t sit still.
  • Part III, Living Beyond Law: science, society, and strategies for uncertain times.

Throughout the book, you run the experiments yourself instead of being told how they turn out.
