## What the reader sees and does
- **The instrument** (top): a tray. Two detector plates stand on the two ends of a scale from 0 to 1; 12 cobalt nuclei (3 × 4) sit between them. Each nucleus is a ball with a ring round it, like Saturn: the back of the ring passes behind the ball, and an arrowhead rides round the ring, so it is seen to turn (Dan asked for spin without 3D). Warm, they jiggle and tilt every which way.
- **Temperature** slider, log scale, 300 K → 0.003 K. At 0.003 K a 12-second clock starts ("Quick, we can't stay near absolute zero for long!"). When it runs out, the crystal warms by itself (about 4 s), the nuclei scatter, counting stops, and the reader may cool it again.
- **Magnet**, then **Reverse** (one button; filled once on). Cold plus magnet: 7 of the 12 nuclei (58%, about Wu's 60%) turn so their spins point left; the rest stay every which way. Reverse: the lined-up spins turn round to point right.
- **Catch electrons.** Electrons fly left or right out of the nuclei, slowly enough to catch (1.4 s across the open space by hand). Tap one to catch it (orange, with a small ring). Keyboard: Enter or Space catches the electron that has flown furthest. A caught electron that reaches its plate **lights the plate** briefly and sends a **pulse** down the plate and along the scale to the **orange pointer**, which slides to the share flying right. Uncaught electrons pass uncounted.
- **Run**: a detector counts **10,000 electrons in about 8 s** (as many as The Lawful Coin's coins; Dan, 2026-11-25), or until the crystal warms. Only a few are drawn, flying and lighting the plates (the status says so, as the gas chamber's "400 drawn" does); the pointer follows the count. Pulses run only for the reader's own catches. Each Run starts a new record; earlier records stay, faint (as in The Lawful Coin). Pressing Run while the nuclei are still swinging into line waits for the magnet, then starts.
- Tallies at the plates, in the accent: "← 37" and "81 →".
- **A Run counts on through the warm-up** (Dan, 2026-11-25: "the pointer still goes, but moves towards the 50/50"), as Wu's did: as the nuclei lose their alignment, new electrons stop leaning, and the running share drifts back toward ½ (it bends back; it doesn't snap, since the earlier counts stay in it). Status: warmingRun ("Warming up: the nuclei scatter, and new electrons stop leaning."), then faded ("Warmed mid-run: the lean faded, and the share drifted back toward half."). The margin note beside the widget tells it: the team cooled the crystal again run after run, and the lopsidedness faded as it warmed (Dan, 2026-11-25). The caption is unchanged. Catching is allowed during the warm-up too.
- **Failed runs are the control, not a glitch** (Dan, 2026-11-25: "It shows how difficult it is to do an experiment right"). A warm or jumbled run stays inside the fair coin's bands, and the status line says why; a Run the warm-up overtakes bends back toward ½ and says so. As in Wu's run, where the lopsidedness faded as the crystal warmed. "Experiments are hard" is told in a margin note beside the widget, not by extra hurdles.
- Dragging the slider during the warm-up takes over at once (before, the drag was ignored and the reader could unknowingly run a warm crystal).
- **The record** (below, `core/strip.js`, same scale): the share flying right, traced down as electrons are counted (count axis 10 → 100 → 1,000 → 10,000; the trace keeps every count to 600, then thins). The fair coin's bands (1 and 2 standard deviations, from `coins.js`) fade in once a record has 40 electrons; "fair coin" marks the middle.
- **A record belongs to one setup**: a new one starts with each Run, and when the setup changes (lined up or not; the magnet's direction; each cold spell).
- Status line, in order of play: start → warm → cooler → quick → lining → tap → keep / running → **lean** ("More fly away from the spin than a fair coin allows.") → Reverse → reversed ("…Which way will the lean go now?") → **crossed** ("The lean crossed over with the spins: away from the spin, as before.") → another lean the same way → **runN** ("A new run, the same shape. Each decay is random; the lean is lawful.", as in the §5 widgets). Unaligned records of 40+ inside the band: **even** (cold) or **warmRun** ("Warm: the nuclei point every which way, and it comes out like a fair coin."). Run before alignment: **wait**. Warm-up: warming; after a cut-short run: **cut** ("The cold ran out mid-run. Cool it again, and run again.").
- Caption: "**Wu's cobalt.** Cool the crystal and line up the nuclei. Catch electrons, or Run: each hit nudges the pointer, and the share settles outside a fair coin's bands." (158 characters)

## Honesty (each sentence rests on a test)
- **Real:** 0.003 K; cold held ~15 min (compressed to 12 s); at most ~60% lined up; more electrons opposite the spin, and reversing the field reversed the asymmetry (Wu et al. 1957; SEP fig. 2; NIST SP 958).
- **Illustrative:** the size of the lean (a lined-up nucleus sends 75% away from its spin), the 0.1 K onset, the time scales.
- **Verdicts:** "lean" only when away − toward exceeds 2√n with n ≥ 40, in a lined-up record; "even" only when |right − left| ≤ 2√n with n ≥ 40 (inside the outer band ⇔ "even", tested). Lined up, a full Run (10,000) ends outside the outer band every time; even a Run cut to 1 s shows the lean in ≥ 99%. Warm or jumbled, a full Run stays inside in ≥ 93% (a fair coin: about 95%). A Run fits one cold spell. Wu et al. (1957): the asymmetry disappeared as the sample warmed, together with the polarization (warm-up about 6 min); Fig. 2 shows the polarizing field up and down.

## Behaviour
Starts moving on view; under reduced motion it waits for the reader's first move. The instrument is a tab stop (Enter catches). Counts read to screen readers every 3 s; the record's label carries the count and latest share. Fits one phone screen: about 680–690 px at 360–390 px; buttons in one row.
